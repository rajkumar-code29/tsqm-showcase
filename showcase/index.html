6

The operating company · for investors

Queen Mary Cruises

The one you asked to be able to put in front of VCs. The menu is the Cunard and Belmond structure — Voyages, The Ship, Staterooms, Dining, Private Charter — because an investor reads a navigation before they read a word.

It includes a booking system that works. Choose a voyage, a date, a stateroom and guests, and watch the price build line by line. Children are half fare. Confirm it and you get a booking reference. Nothing is charged and nothing leaves your browser — but it answers the question an investor always asks, which is what a customer actually sees.

There is also an investor section with the revenue model, and every forward-looking image is labelled an artist’s impression.

What these are, and what they are not

These are real, working websites. They run on any phone or laptop, they work on a keyboard, and they are quick on a slow connection — the fonts are served from the site itself rather than from Google, so nothing depends on anyone else being up.

What they are not is finished. The photographs are drawings, because I do not have the archive yet. The history is accurate and taken from the public record. The fundraising figures are the ones in your brief and need confirming before anything goes live.

About the hidden page

The code is 1234 and it is checked in your browser, so what the demonstration shows is the experience, not the security.

The real thing works differently, and the difference is the point:

  • No password at all — access is a single-use link sent to a named person
  • To everyone else the address returns an ordinary “page not found”, identical to any mistyped URL
  • Photographs are never files on the internet; they are served one at a time, to one person
  • Each image carries that viewer’s name and the time they opened it
  • Every request is logged, and nobody can edit that log
