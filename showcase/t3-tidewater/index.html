Her story

Ninety years on one river

Built here, worked here, lost for forty years, and brought home.

1933

Launched at Dumbarton

Built by William Denny and Brothers for Williamson-Buchanan Steamers. Forty-four seasons followed.

2016

Home to the Clyde

She came up the firth on 15 May under tow, forty years after she last sailed it. People stood on the banks to watch.

Now

Lifting the deck

Her teak is coming up plank by plank, sorted on the quayside, and relaid. Everything sound is kept.

Next

Back on the water

Steel, boilers, and the certification that lets a 1933 hull carry passengers again.

The vision

There are no others

Two hundred and fifty turbine steamers were built on this river. She is the last one left — not the last in Scotland, the last anywhere. National Historic Ships lists her as nationally pre-eminent.

When she goes, that is the end of it.

Sailing again

Back under her own power on the water she was built for, with the certification a passenger vessel needs in 2027.

A training ship

The first cadet training ship in the UK, in a city that used to build one of these a fortnight.

Open to everyone

Alongside in Glasgow when she is not sailing, for school groups and anyone who remembers her.
