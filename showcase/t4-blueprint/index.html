SHIP’S RECORD

Ninety years, logged

YearEntryNote
1933LaunchedBuilt by William Denny and Brothers, Dumbarton, for Williamson-Buchanan Steamers. Direct-drive turbines.
1935RenamedBecame Queen Mary II so Cunard could use the name for their Clydebank liner. Cunard sent a portrait of the Queen in exchange.
1939RequisitionedWithdrawn from excursion service for the duration; tender and ferry work on the Clyde.
1957Re-boileredConverted from coal to oil. The single funnel dates from this refit.
1976Name restoredTS Queen Mary again, renamed by the actor Roddy McMillan.
1977WithdrawnForty-four seasons. The last turbine steamer working the Clyde.
1988RelocatedThames, Waterloo Bridge, as a bar and restaurant. Twenty years.
2015AcquiredFriends of TS Queen Mary take ownership, 21 October, after raising some £300,000.
2016RepatriatedTowed up the Firth of Clyde, 15 May, forty years after she last sailed it.

SCOPE OF WORKS

What the money buys

Concrete items, not categories. A funder can price every line.

W/01

Steel decks

Replacing plate open to Clyde weather for forty years. The largest single structural item.

W/02

Boilers and machinery

The turbines are the reason she matters. Getting them turning again is the largest line in the budget.

W/03

Passenger certification

A 1933 hull must satisfy 2027 safety rules. Unglamorous, expensive, non-negotiable.
